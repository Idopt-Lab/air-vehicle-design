Generated 2026-08-03. Run at Brandt's own W_TO=31377.00 lbf, W_energy=6296.30 lbf (Wt!B3/B38, Wt!B6).

**Reference** (the `Reference` and `%Diff` columns): VnV/BrandtF16A/GroundTruth/f16a_subsystems_ground_truth.json (fuel/avionics volume, landing-gear tire diameter) and f16a_ground_truth.json .weights (fuel/avionics WEIGHT, cross-check rows only).

**2nd Source** (the `2nd Source` column): Nicolai & Carichner's independently-published "nose gear ~20% of TOGW" rule of thumb (p.202) -- a THIRD data point for the gear-load-split question, alongside Raymer's 90/10 (used) and Brandt's derived 26.7/73.3 (not used).

This is a **comparison report, not a test** — no pass/fail assertions, not part of `run_all_tests`, and **nothing here may ever be used to backfill a unit test's expected value**. Where a quantity has more than one valid implementation, each option gets its own row, and rejected variants are reported too so the decisions stay auditable.

**Reading the `Divergence` column.** `BY DESIGN` — the framework computes the *same kind* of quantity as the reference but is *expected* to differ (a different formula family, or a locked decision to follow a physical/T.O. value); a large %Diff there is the correct answer, not a defect, though its magnitude is worth a sanity check. `DEFINITIONAL` — the two sides are *different kinds of quantity altogether*, so the %Diff is not an error measure at all; do not chase it. **Blank** — a genuine agreement check, where a large %Diff **is** worth chasing.

**Most rows in this report are honestly "NOT MODELED" against Brandt**, not because the framework fails to compute fuel/avionics volume (it does, at L2/L3), but because Brandt's own workbook never ground-truths a VOLUME for either quantity -- only WEIGHTS exist there, and this report does not convert one to the other (that would require the same uncited fuel density the weights side already flags as an open gap). See docs/subplans/09_subsystems.md "Ground Truth" section.

**Landing-gear tire diameter is the one quantity with a real Brandt data point**, but it carries TWO caveats this report deliberately does not hide: (1) the Brandt gear block's only citation is an undocumented "Gear tab" reference with no cell letter, omitted from readme_geom.md and cell-map.md; (2) the SAME gear block implies a 26.7%/73.3% main/nose load split that nearly reverses both Raymer's typical-fighter 90/10 (used here by design) and Nicolai's own 20%-nose rule of thumb. VnV/BrandtF16A/todo.md, 2026-07-31 entry, has the full record -- not resolved here, not resolved by this report.

**Two equation items ship as documented citation gaps, not comparison rows**: battery-electric volumetric energy density (item 7) and landing-gear bay-volume packaging (item 11). Both are pinned by deliberately-labeled Tier-1 unit tests (testTODO_*), not resolved or estimated here.
