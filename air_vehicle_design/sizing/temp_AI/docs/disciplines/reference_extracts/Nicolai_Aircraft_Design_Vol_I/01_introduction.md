## Figures and Graphs

### Fig 1.13 — Empty weight reduction by eliminating all crew station equipment and shortening fuselage — **DATA GRAPH (tabulated)**
*[Nicolai & Carichner, Fig. 1.13, p. 18]*

- **X-axis:** Initial Empty Weight (1000 lb), 0–50
- **Y-axis:** Empty Weight Reduction (%), 0–12
- Two trend curves: **Single Seat** and **Two Seat**. Values *(read from plot, approximate)*:

| Aircraft | Category | Initial Empty Weight (1000 lb) | Empty Weight Reduction (%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| F-5E    | Single seat | ~10   | ~10.0 |
| AV-8B   | Single seat | ~11.5 | ~8.3 |
| A-7A    | Single seat | ~16   | ~8.5 |
| U-2S    | Single seat | ~14.5 | ~5.4 |
| F-16C   | Single seat | ~20   | ~6.3 |
| F/A-18A | Single seat | ~20.5 | ~5.0 |
| F-15C   | Single seat | ~27   | ~4.7 |
| F-117A  | Single seat | ~27.5 | ~4.8 |
| A-6E    | Two seat    | ~26   | ~8.5 |
| F-14A   | Two seat    | ~40   | ~6.3 |
| F-111F  | Two seat    | ~47   | ~6.0 |

> Trend: eliminating the crew station yields ~10% empty-weight reduction for small
> single-seat fighters, falling toward ~5–6% as size increases; the two-seat curve
> is offset higher (bigger crew-station fraction removed) but also decreases with size.

### Fig 1.14 — The three phases or levels of aircraft design — **STRUCTURED PHASE DIAGRAM**
*[Nicolai & Carichner, Fig. 1.14, p. 23]* (Ref. [12])

| Row | Phase 1: Conceptual Design | Phase 2: Preliminary Design | Phase 3: Detail Design |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Known** | Basic Mission Requirements (Range, Altitude, Speed); Basic Material Properties (σ/ρ, E/ρ, $/lb) | Aeroelastic Reqs; Fatigue Reqs; Flutter Reqs; Overall Strength Reqs | Local Strength Reqs; Producibility; Functional Reqs |
| **Results** | Geometry (Airfoil Type, R, t/c, λ, Λ); Design Objectives (Drag Level, Weight Goals, Cost Goals) | Basic Internal Arrangement; Complete External Configuration (Camber & Twist Distribution, Local Flow Problems Solved); Major Loads, Stresses, Deflections | Detail Design (Mechanisms, Joint Fittings and Attachments); Design Refinements as Result of Testing |
| **Output** | Feasible Design | Mature Design | Shop Designs |
| **TRL** | 2–3 | 4–5 | 6–7 |

### Fig 1.16 — Design phases integrated into the entire government program — **DATA GRAPH + schedule (tabulated)**
*[Nicolai & Carichner, Fig. 1.16, p. 27]*

Top plot — **Cum Percent vs Years** (0–10 yr, 0–100%). Two curves *(read from plot, approximate)*:

| Years | LCC Committed (%) | Actual Funds Spent (%) |
|---|---|---|
| 0   | 0   | 0  |
| 1   | ~70 | ~2 |
| 1.5 | ~77 (data pt) | ~4 |
| 2   | ~82 | ~5 |
| 2.7 | ~86 (data pt) | ~9 |
| 4   | ~91 (data pt) | ~18 |
| 5.6 | ~95 (data pt) | ~35 |
| 6   | ~96 | ~42 |
| 8   | ~99 | ~72 |
| 10  | ~100 | ~100 |

- Banner: "Design to Cost" (early) transitioning to "Manage to Cost" (later).
- Key point (per text, p. 27): **over 70% of the design features that drive LCC are committed during the conceptual design phase**, while actual funds are spent much later.

Schedule bars below the plot (approximate year spans, *read from plot*):
- **Design Phases:** Concept ~0.5–2 yr; PD ~1.5–3; Detail ~2.7–4; Development ~3.5–5.5; Production ~4.5–7.
- **Program spans:** RDT&E ~1–5.5 yr; Production ~5–8; Operations ~6–10.
- **Acquisition Schedules (DoD 5000.1/.2), by era:**
  - 1970s: Conceptual → Validation → FSD → Production; milestones 0, 1, 2, 3, IOC.
  - 1990s: Concept Exploration → PDRR → EMD → Production.
  - 2000s: Concept Refine / Tech Dev → System Dev & Demo → Production & Deployment → Operation & Support; milestones A, B, C, IOC.
